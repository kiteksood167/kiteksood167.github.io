## Step 1: Pick the right capacity

**10,000mAh** is enough for most people. It's light enough for a pocket or small bag and will usually give a mid-range phone roughly one full charge, with a little left over.

**20,000mAh** suits long travel days, two devices, or heavy users who game or navigate on the go. Expect around two phone charges, but also a heavier, bulkier bank (about 420–490g).

**25,000mAh and above** is for trips where you won't see a plug for a while. These are heavy, and you should check the watt-hour (Wh) rating before flying (more on that below).

Remember that the mAh on the box is measured at the battery's internal voltage. Some energy is lost when the bank converts it for your phone and as heat, so a 10,000mAh bank won't deliver 10,000mAh into your phone. Treat the number as a rough guide, not an exact promise.

## Step 2: Match the watts to your device

Watts decide how fast a power bank charges your phone. Most phones under ₹30,000 charge fast at 18–33W from a power bank that supports USB Power Delivery (PD) or PPS. A higher-watt bank won't hurt your phone, as the phone only takes what it can handle, but you won't gain speed either.

Higher wattage starts to matter if you want to charge a tablet or a laptop. Thin laptops usually need 45–65W, and bigger ones need more. Our [fast charging explainer](/fast-charging-explained/) covers USB-C PD and charger standards in more detail.

## Step 3: Check ports, cable and display

A USB-C port that works both ways (charging the bank and your phone) is now the minimum. Built-in cables are handy because you'll never forget one at home. A small display showing the exact percentage left is more useful than four LED dots, especially on larger banks.

Also look for the BIS registration number printed on the box or body. Power banks sold in India need it, and it's a simple way to avoid cheap, unsafe copies sold through random listings.

> **Tip:** Since early 2026, DGCA rules don't allow you to use a power bank to charge devices during a flight in India, or to plug one into the seat socket. Carry it in your cabin bag only, never in checked luggage, and keep it out of the overhead bin. Banks up to 100Wh are fine without airline approval, so check the Wh figure printed on large-capacity models before you fly.
