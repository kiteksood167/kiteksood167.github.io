## 1. LG C6 OLED: best overall picture

If you want the best all-round picture and can stretch your budget, the **LG C6** is the one to get. Like every OLED, each pixel makes its own light, so blacks are truly black and there's no glow around bright objects on a dark background. That makes it a great choice for movie nights in a dim room.

The 2026 model raises the refresh rate to **165Hz** (up from 144Hz on last year's C5) and keeps **four HDMI 2.1 ports**, so a PS5, Xbox Series X and gaming PC can all be plugged in at full speed. It supports Dolby Vision and Dolby Atmos, plus AirPlay 2 and Google Cast for sending video from your phone.

The 65-inch model launched at around $2,700, but it has been selling for around $1,400 (November 2026) at Amazon, Best Buy and LG in early Black Friday deals.

**Downsides:** OLEDs don't get as bright as the best mini-LED TVs, so a very sunny room can wash the picture out a little. It also costs noticeably more than the other picks here.

## 2. Hisense U7SG: best for bright rooms and gaming

The **Hisense U7SG** replaces the old U8 as Hisense's main mid-range TV, and it's a strong pick if your living room gets a lot of daylight. It uses a mini-LED backlight with local dimming, and Hisense rates it at up to 3,000 nits of peak brightness, far more than a typical OLED.

It also has a native **165Hz** panel, **four HDMI 2.1 ports**, Dolby Vision IQ, HDR10+, IMAX Enhanced and a built-in 2.1.2-channel speaker system. Google TV handles apps, and it supports ATSC 3.0 (NextGen TV) broadcasts in the US.

The 65-inch model is listed at around $1,500 but was selling for around $1,000 (November 2026) on Amazon.

**Downsides:** like most LCD-based TVs, it can't match an OLED for perfect blacks or wide viewing angles. In the UK and Australia, Hisense uses its own VIDAA software instead of Google TV, so check the exact model before buying outside the US.

## 3. TCL QM7L: best value

For most people, the **TCL QM7L** is the best balance of price and picture. It uses TCL's 2026 "SQD" mini-LED panel with up to 2,100 local dimming zones, which keeps light from spilling around bright objects much better than a basic LED TV.

TechRadar's review measured about 2,270 to 2,400 nits on a small bright window, which is plenty for daytime viewing and punchy HDR highlights. It runs at **144Hz**, supports Dolby Vision IQ, HDR10+ and AMD FreeSync Premium Pro, and has Bang & Olufsen-tuned speakers with Dolby Atmos and DTS:X.

Best Buy lists the 65-inch model at $1,499.99, but it was on sale for around $900 (November 2026).

**Downsides:** only two of its four HDMI ports are HDMI 2.1, so if you have more than two consoles or a gaming PC you'll need to swap cables or use a receiver. Reviewers also found its built-in Gemini assistant hit-and-miss.

If you want a big screen for as little as possible, last year's **TCL QM6K** is still one of the best cheap 65-inch TVs around. Despite its low price, it has a quantum dot mini-LED panel, a 144Hz refresh rate, two HDMI 2.1 ports, and support for Dolby Vision and HDR10+. Tom's Guide rates it as its top 65-inch pick under $500.

It's a 2025 model, so stock is starting to thin out, but that also means it gets steep discounts. Its list price is around $800, and it dropped to around $530 at several big retailers during 2026 sales (June 2026). Wait for a similar price on Black Friday rather than paying list price.

**Downsides:** it's not as bright or as precise as the QM7L, and the built-in speakers are basic, so a soundbar is worth budgeting for.

## How to choose the right 65-inch TV

- **OLED or mini-LED?** OLED gives the best contrast and viewing angles, and suits darker rooms. Mini-LED gets much brighter and costs less, which suits bright rooms and daytime sports.
- **Refresh rate and HDMI 2.1:** if you have a PS5 or Xbox Series X, look for at least 120Hz and HDMI 2.1. All four picks here qualify.
- **HDR formats:** Dolby Vision is used by Netflix, Disney+ and Apple TV. Prime Video also uses HDR10+. A TV that supports both covers the most content.
- **Viewing distance:** a 65-inch 4K TV looks best from roughly 6.5 to 9 feet away. Much closer and you may notice the screen edges; much further and a 75-inch could be worth a look.
- **Sound:** most thin TVs have average speakers. Even a basic soundbar makes dialogue easier to hear.
