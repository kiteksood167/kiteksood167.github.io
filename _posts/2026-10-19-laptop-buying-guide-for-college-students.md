A college laptop needs to survive four years of assignments, online classes, late-night Netflix and maybe some coding or design work. Spend too little and it slows down by the second year. Spend too much and you pay for gaming features you'll never use.

Here's how to pick the right one for your course and budget.

<!--more-->

## Step 1: Match the laptop to your course

| Your course | What you'll run | What to prioritise |
|---|---|---|
| Arts, commerce, law, BBA | Docs, browser, Zoom, Excel | Battery, light weight, good keyboard |
| Engineering (CS/IT) | Coding tools, VS Code, virtual machines | 16GB RAM, fast SSD, good processor |
| Engineering (Mech/Civil) | AutoCAD, SolidWorks, MATLAB | Dedicated graphics card, 16GB RAM |
| Design, media, architecture | Photoshop, Premiere, Blender | Dedicated GPU, colour-accurate screen |

If you're unsure, ask a senior in your course what software they actually use in later years.

## Step 2: Processor

For most students, a recent **Intel Core i5 / Core Ultra 5** or **AMD Ryzen 5** is the sweet spot. Core i3 / Ryzen 3 is fine for basic course work only. Core i7 / Ryzen 7 only makes sense for heavy design, video or engineering software.

**Watch the generation.** A new i5 is often faster than an old i7. Search the full processor name, such as "Ryzen 5 7530U", and compare it with others on your list.

## Step 3: RAM — 16GB if you can

8GB works for browsing and documents, but it gets tight with many Chrome tabs and Teams open. **16GB** will keep the laptop smooth for all four years. If you buy 8GB, check whether the RAM is **upgradeable** (has a free slot or is not soldered). That lets you add more later for a small cost.

## Step 4: Storage — SSD only

Never buy a laptop with a hard disk (HDD) as its main drive. A **512GB NVMe SSD** is the right size for most students. It makes the laptop boot in seconds and open apps instantly.

## Step 5: Screen

- **Size:** 14 inches is easy to carry; 15.6 inches is better for long study sessions at a desk.
- **Resolution:** Full HD (1920×1080) minimum.
- **Panel:** Choose **IPS** or **OLED**. Avoid "TN" panels, which look washed out from angles.
- **Brightness:** 300 nits or more if you'll use it near windows.

## Step 6: Battery, weight and build

If you carry it to college every day, aim for under **1.6 kg** and a real-world battery of 6+ hours. Brand claims are usually optimistic, so check reviews that test battery with normal use.

## Step 7: The extras people forget

  <img width="1200" height="675" src="/assets/images/laptop-ports-usb-hdmi.jpg" alt="A laptop's USB-C, USB-A and HDMI ports labelled" loading="lazy">

- **Free MS Office:** Many laptops sold in India include Office Home & Student, which is worth several thousand rupees.
- **Warranty:** Consider an extended warranty or accidental damage protection. Students drop laptops more than they'd like to admit.
- **Student discounts:** Some brands have education stores with lower prices if you verify your college email.
- **Ports:** At least one USB-C, two USB-A and HDMI saves you from buying dongles.

> **Simple rule:** For most students, a recent i5/Ryzen 5 + 16GB RAM + 512GB SSD + Full HD IPS screen is the best value for money. Add a dedicated GPU only if your course software needs it.

## The bottom line

Buy for your course, not for the spec sheet. Prioritise 16GB RAM and an SSD, check upgradeability, and take the warranty seriously. A well-chosen mid-range laptop will easily last your whole degree.
