If your phone struggles to last the day, you don't always need a new one. A few settings changes can add hours to battery life, and a few habits can keep your battery healthy for years. Here are the tips that actually work, and a few myths to stop believing.

<!--more-->

## Part 1: Settings that save battery today

### 1. Find the battery-hungry apps first
Go to **Settings → Battery** and see which apps use the most. Often one app, like a social media app or a game running in the background, is responsible for most of the drain. Restrict its background activity or uninstall it.

### 2. Lower screen brightness and use auto-brightness
The screen is usually the single biggest drain. Turn on **adaptive/auto brightness** and lower the default slider a little.

### 3. Use dark mode on AMOLED screens
On AMOLED/OLED phones, black pixels are switched off completely, so **dark mode** saves real power. On LCD screens, it makes little difference.

### 4. Reduce the screen timeout
Set the screen to turn off after **30 seconds** of inactivity instead of 2 minutes or more.

### 5. Turn down the refresh rate when you need to
A 120Hz screen uses more power than 60Hz. If your phone has an **adaptive** refresh rate option, use it. On a long travel day, switching to 60Hz can noticeably stretch your battery.

### 6. Limit background location
Many apps ask for location "all the time". Change most of them to **"Only while using the app"** under Settings → Location → App permissions.

### 7. Check your signal
In areas with weak network signal, your phone works harder to stay connected, which drains the battery fast. When you're on good Wi-Fi in a low-signal area, turning on Wi-Fi calling helps. Use flight mode if you don't need the network at all.

### 8. Turn on battery saver early
Don't wait until 5%. Turning on battery saver at **20–30%** gives you a lot more usable time.

## Part 2: Habits that keep your battery healthy for years

### 9. Avoid heat

<figure class="inline-figure">
  <img width="1200" height="675" src="/assets/images/phone-overheating-car.jpg" alt="A phone overheating on a car dashboard in the sun" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>Illustration</figcaption>
</figure>
Heat is the number one enemy of lithium-ion batteries. Don't leave your phone in a hot car or in direct sun, and avoid heavy gaming **while charging**. Take off a thick case if the phone gets hot while charging.

### 10. Keep it between 20% and 80% when you can
Batteries age faster when they are kept at 100% or drained to 0% often. Many phones now have an **"optimised charging"** or **"protect battery"** option that limits charging to around 80%. Turn it on, especially if you charge overnight.

### 11. Use a good charger
Use the original charger or a certified one from a trusted brand. Cheap unbranded chargers can deliver unstable power, which can harm the battery and is a safety risk.

### 12. Keep your phone updated
Software updates often include battery optimisations and bug fixes for drain problems. Install them, ideally after reading a few early reviews to make sure there are no new bugs.

## Myths to stop believing

- **"Close all apps from recents to save battery."** Usually false. Reopening apps from scratch can use *more* power than resuming them. Only close apps that are misbehaving.
- **"Charging overnight ruins the battery."** Modern phones stop charging at full. Turning on optimised charging makes it even safer.
- **"Fast charging damages the battery."** Phones manage fast charging carefully, and the speed drops as the battery fills. Heat is the bigger concern, not the charger speed.

> **When is it time for a new battery?** If your phone shows battery health below about 80%, or it shuts down at 15–20%, a battery replacement at an authorised service centre is usually much cheaper than a new phone.

## The bottom line

Find the app draining your battery, lower brightness, use dark mode on AMOLED, limit background location and turn on battery saver early. For long-term health, avoid heat and use the 80% charging limit.
