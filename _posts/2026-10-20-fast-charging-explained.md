Your phone says "67W fast charging", but with your friend's charger it crawls. You buy a "65W" charger, and it's still not fast. Fast charging is confusing because brands use different standards. This guide explains it simply, so you can pick the right charger the first time.

<!--more-->

## Watts: the basic idea

Charging speed is measured in **watts (W)**. More watts usually means faster charging, but only if **the phone, the charger and the cable all support the same speed and the same standard**. The slowest part decides the final speed.

## The three main fast-charging types

| Type | Used by | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| **USB-C Power Delivery (PD)** | iPhones, Google Pixel, many laptops, and many Android phones | The universal standard. A good PD charger works with most modern devices. |
| **PD with PPS** | Samsung "Super Fast Charging" and several others | An extension of PD. Your charger must support **PPS** to reach the top speed. |
| **Brand-only (proprietary)** | Many Chinese phone brands' highest speeds | Full speed usually needs the **original charger and cable** from that brand. |

This explains the common problem: a phone rated for very high-wattage proprietary charging may only charge at a much lower speed on a standard PD charger, because the top speed needs the brand's own system.

## Why is my phone charging slowly?

- **Wrong standard:** the charger doesn't support the phone's fast-charging type.
- **Weak cable:** cheap or old cables often can't carry high power. Cables for 60W and above need to be rated for it.
- **Heat:** phones slow down charging when they're warm, and while you're gaming.
- **Battery level:** charging is fastest from low battery and slows down a lot after about 80%. That's normal and protects the battery.
- **Battery protection features:** optimised or adaptive charging may pause at 80% on purpose.

## How to choose the right charger

  <img src="/assets/images/usb-c-braided-cable.jpg" alt="Close-up of a braided USB-C cable plug" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>Illustration</figcaption>
</figure>

1. **Check your phone's standard** in the specs or the manual: PD, PPS, or a brand-only system.
2. **If your phone uses a brand-only system,** the original brand charger is the only way to get top speed.
3. **For everything else,** a quality **USB-C PD charger with PPS** is the most flexible choice. It can charge your phone, earbuds and many laptops.
4. **Match the wattage** to your fastest device. A 30–45W PD charger covers most phones, while laptops typically need 65W or more.
5. **Use a rated cable,** and check its wattage rating, especially for laptops.

> **Tip:** Avoid very cheap unbranded chargers. They can deliver unstable power, overheat, and in the worst cases damage your phone or be a fire risk. Buy from known brands with safety certifications.

## Is fast charging bad for the battery?

Modern phones manage fast charging carefully: speeds drop as the battery fills and as temperature rises. **Heat** is the bigger enemy of battery health, not the charger itself. Charge in a cool place, avoid gaming while charging, and use your phone's battery protection setting if you charge overnight.
