TV shopping is full of confusing labels: 4K, QLED, Mini LED, OLED, HDR10+, Dolby Vision, 120Hz. Some of these make a huge difference to what you see, and some are mostly marketing. This guide explains which ones matter, so you can choose the right TV for your room and budget.

<!--more-->

## 1. Size: bigger is usually better, within reason

<figure class="inline-figure">
  <img src="/assets/images/tv-viewing-distance.jpg" alt="A person on a sofa with the viewing distance to a large TV marked" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>Illustration</figcaption>
</figure>

A simple rule: measure the distance from your sofa to the TV wall.

| Viewing distance | Comfortable 4K TV size |
|---|---|
| About 1.5–2 m | 43–50 inches |
| About 2–2.5 m | 55 inches |
| About 2.5–3 m | 65 inches |
| More than 3 m | 75 inches or bigger |

Most people who upgrade say they wish they'd gone one size bigger.

## 2. Resolution: 4K is the standard

At 43 inches and above, choose **4K (Ultra HD)**. Full HD is only worth it on small, cheaper TVs. **8K** TVs exist, but there's very little 8K content, so they aren't worth the extra money for most people.

## 3. Panel type: the biggest factor for picture quality

| Panel | Picture | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **LED (LCD)** | Good. Blacks can look greyish in dark rooms. | Budget buyers, bright rooms |
| **QLED** | Brighter, more vivid colours than basic LED | Bright living rooms |
| **Mini LED** | Much better contrast and brightness than regular LED | Movies and sports, mid-to-high budget |
| **OLED** | Perfect blacks and excellent contrast | Dark rooms and movie lovers, premium budget |

## 4. HDR: check which formats are supported

HDR makes bright scenes pop and dark scenes show detail, but **only if the TV is bright enough**. Look for support for **HDR10**, plus **Dolby Vision** or **HDR10+**, which streaming apps use. A cheap TV that "supports HDR" but isn't bright won't show much difference.

## 5. Refresh rate: 120Hz for gamers and sports fans

- **60Hz** is fine for TV shows, news and most movies.
- **120Hz** gives smoother sports and gaming. If you have a modern console, look for **120Hz plus HDMI 2.1**.
- Watch out for "motion rate" numbers in ads. They're often not the real refresh rate.

## 6. Smart TV software

The operating system decides your apps and how smooth the TV feels. **Google TV / Android TV**, **Tizen** (Samsung) and **webOS** (LG) all support the major streaming apps. Check that the apps you use, like Netflix, Prime Video, JioHotstar and YouTube, are supported, and that the TV feels quick in store or in video reviews.

## 7. Sound: plan for a soundbar

<figure class="inline-figure">
  <img src="/assets/images/tv-soundbar.jpg" alt="A slim soundbar below a TV with sound waves" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>Illustration</figcaption>
</figure>

Thin TVs have small speakers. If you care about dialogue clarity and movie sound, budget for a **soundbar**, even an affordable one. It's usually a bigger upgrade than spending more on the TV's built-in audio.

> **Tip:** Check the brand's service network in your city before buying, and consider the extended warranty for panels. A TV panel repair can cost a large part of the TV's price.

## The bottom line

Pick the size for your room, go **4K**, and spend your money on a better **panel**, such as QLED, Mini LED or OLED, before chasing extra features. Check HDR format support, choose **120Hz with HDMI 2.1** if you game, and plan for a soundbar. A good panel and smooth software matter far more than the longest spec list.
