In the US, UK and many other countries, you can buy a phone in two ways: directly from your mobile carrier, often with monthly payments, or "unlocked" from a store or the manufacturer. The carrier deal usually looks cheaper on day one. Whether it actually is depends on how long you keep the phone and how much freedom you want.

<!--more-->

## What "locked" and "unlocked" mean

A **carrier-locked** phone only works with the network that sold it, at least until it's unlocked. An **unlocked** phone works with any compatible carrier: you can switch providers, use a cheaper prepaid plan, or put in a local SIM when you travel.

Most phones sold directly by manufacturers and big retailers are unlocked. Phones sold by carriers, especially with monthly payment plans or big discounts, are usually locked for a period of time.

## The real comparison

| | Carrier phone | Unlocked phone |
|---|---|---|
| **Upfront cost** | Low or zero, spread over 24–36 months | Full price upfront, or split with a store payment plan |
| **Deals** | Big trade-in and "free phone" offers | Occasional sales, fewer huge discounts |
| **Freedom to switch** | Limited until the phone is paid off or unlocked | Switch carriers any time |
| **Plan cost** | Deals often require more expensive plans | Use the cheapest plan that suits you |
| **Travel** | Local SIMs may not work until unlocked | Use local or eSIM data plans abroad |
| **Pre-installed apps** | Often includes carrier apps | Usually cleaner software |

## When a carrier deal makes sense

- **The discount is large and genuine.** Trade-in deals on new flagships can save hundreds of dollars or pounds.
- **You were going to stay with that carrier anyway** for the whole contract or payment period.
- **You need to spread the cost** and the payment plan has no interest.

Read the small print carefully. Many "free phone" offers are paid as monthly credits, so if you leave early, you lose the remaining credits and may owe the rest of the phone's price.

- **You want a cheaper plan.** Prepaid and smaller carriers (often called MVNOs) usually cost far less than the big carriers' premium plans, and they run on the same networks.
- **You travel often.** An unlocked phone with eSIM support lets you buy cheap local data in minutes.
- **You keep phones a long time** or resell them. Unlocked phones are easier to resell and often fetch higher prices.
- **You want fewer strings attached**, with no lock-in, no surprise fees when switching, and less bloatware.

## How to work out the true cost

  <img src="/assets/images/unlocked-phone-true-cost.jpg" alt="A phone next to a calculator and a monthly phone bill in dollars with a True cost note" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>Illustration</figcaption>

Add up everything over two years for both options:

1. **Phone price**, minus any trade-in or discount you'll *actually* receive
2. **24 months of the plan** the deal requires
3. **Any fees**, such as activation or upgrade fees

Then do the same for the unlocked phone plus the cheapest plan that meets your needs. The cheaper total is the better deal. For many people, an unlocked phone on a budget plan wins, even when the carrier deal looked better at first.

> **Tip:** Before buying any phone, check it supports your carrier's network bands and eSIM. Phones made for one region can miss bands used in another country, which means weaker signal or slower data.

## Buying unlocked: what to check

- **"Unlocked" in the listing**, from the manufacturer or a trusted retailer, not just "compatible with" one carrier
- **The model number matches your region**, so it supports local bands and gets proper warranty support
- **eSIM support**, which makes switching carriers and travelling much easier
- **The return policy**, in case the phone doesn't work well on your network

## The bottom line

A carrier deal can be worth it if the discount is big and you'd stay with that carrier anyway. For most people who want freedom, cheaper plans and easy travel, an unlocked phone on a budget plan saves more money over two years. Always compare the total two-year cost, not just the price of the phone on day one.
