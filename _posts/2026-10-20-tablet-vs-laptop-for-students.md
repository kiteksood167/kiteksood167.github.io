Many students today wonder whether a tablet can replace a laptop. For some courses it absolutely can, and it's lighter, cheaper and lasts longer on battery. For others, a tablet will frustrate you by the second semester. Here's how to tell which group you're in.

<!--more-->

## Where tablets are better

- **Handwritten notes and diagrams:** with a stylus, a tablet is the best digital notebook there is. Great for science, maths, medicine and design students.
- **Reading PDFs and e-books:** the large touchscreen held in your hands is far more comfortable than a laptop.
- **Online classes and videos:** a light device with good speakers and long battery life.
- **Portability:** much lighter than most laptops, and easy to use in a bus, library or bed.
- **Battery life:** many tablets easily last a full day of classes.

## Where laptops are better

- **Typing long assignments and projects:** a proper keyboard and trackpad make a huge difference over hours.
- **Coding and engineering software:** tools like VS Code, MATLAB, AutoCAD and many college lab programs need a full desktop operating system.
- **Multitasking:** several windows side by side, many browser tabs, and drag-and-drop between apps.
- **Online exams and college portals:** some exam software and portals only support Windows or macOS.
- **File management:** easier to organise, download and transfer large files.

## Which is right for your course?

| Your course | Better choice |
|---|---|
| Medicine, biology, chemistry, maths | **Tablet + stylus** (notes and diagrams), laptop optional |
| Arts, humanities, languages | **Tablet with a keyboard case** often works well |
| Commerce, BBA, law | **Laptop** for Excel, presentations and long documents |
| Engineering, computer science, IT | **Laptop**, which is essential for software |
| Design, architecture | **Both** ideally: a tablet for sketching and a laptop for design software |

## What about a tablet with a keyboard?

A tablet with a keyboard case can cover light typing well. But it's still a tablet: some software won't run, the trackpad experience is limited, and the combined price can get close to a budget laptop. If you'll be typing for hours most days, a laptop is usually the smarter buy.

> **Tip:** Before buying, check your college's requirements. Ask a senior which software and exam portals they actually use. One unsupported exam app can force you to buy a laptop anyway.

## Specs to check on a study tablet

- **Screen size:** around 10–11 inches is a good balance of portability and space for split-screen.
- **Stylus support:** check whether a stylus is supported, and whether it's included or sold separately.
- **Storage:** at least 128GB if you'll download lectures and notes.
- **Software updates:** a tablet that gets several years of updates stays useful through your whole course.

## The bottom line

Choose a **tablet** if your course is mostly reading, handwritten notes and online classes. Choose a **laptop** if you'll be typing a lot, coding or using specialised software. If you can only afford one device and you're unsure, a laptop is the safer choice, because it can do everything a student needs, even if it's heavier.
