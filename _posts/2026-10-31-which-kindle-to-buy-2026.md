Amazon refreshed almost its whole Kindle lineup in October 2026: a new 6-inch Kindle, a thinner Paperwhite and a redesigned Colorsoft, each with a pricier Signature Edition. That's six e-readers between about $150 and $320, and the differences aren't obvious. Here's what each one gets you and which is worth buying.

<!--more-->

## The 2026 Kindle lineup at a glance

| | Kindle | Paperwhite | Colorsoft |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price (with ads) | Around $150 | Around $200 | Around $290 |
| Screen | 6-inch, 300 ppi | 7-inch, 300 ppi | 7-inch color: 300 ppi black and white, 150 ppi color |
| Storage | 16GB | 16GB | 16GB |
| Warm light | No | Yes | Yes |
| Waterproof (IPX8) | No | Yes | Yes |
| Battery (Amazon's claim) | Up to 6 weeks | Up to 12 weeks | Up to 8 weeks |
| Signature Edition | Aluminum version, around $190, 32GB | Around $250, 32GB | Around $320, 32GB |

Prices are Amazon US list prices at launch (October 2026). The cheapest Kindle and the base Paperwhite show lockscreen ads; removing them costs extra (for example, the Paperwhite is about $220 without ads). The basic Kindle was on sale from October 1, and the new Paperwhite and Colorsoft were listed to ship on October 28.

## Kindle (6-inch): the cheapest way in

The entry-level Kindle starts at around $150 with ads. Amazon says it's about 1mm thinner than the last one, weighs 140g and opens books 30% faster. The screen is a sharp 300 ppi, so text looks as crisp as on the Paperwhite.

What you give up: there's **no warm light** and **no waterproofing**, and the battery is rated for about 6 weeks instead of 12. There's also a $190 version with an aluminum back and 32GB of storage, mainly for people who want a nicer finish.

**Who it's for:** occasional readers, kids (with a case), or anyone who mostly reads in bed with the lights on and never by the pool.

## Kindle Paperwhite (7-inch): the best Kindle for most people

The Paperwhite costs about $50 more and fixes everything the basic Kindle lacks. You get a bigger 7-inch screen, an adjustable warm light for reading at night, IPX8 waterproofing and a battery rated for up to 12 weeks. TechRadar notes that the 2026 model is thinner than the 2024 version (6.8mm instead of 7.8mm) and slightly lighter, and the power button has moved to a spot where it's harder to press by accident.

The **Signature Edition** (about $250) adds 32GB of storage, an aluminum back, a front light that adjusts itself, dock charging and a double-tap gesture to turn pages. Those are nice extras, not essentials. Unless you store a lot of audiobooks, the regular Paperwhite is enough.

**Who it's for:** almost everyone who reads regularly, especially at night, in the bath or on vacation.

## Kindle Colorsoft (7-inch): color, at a price

The Colorsoft is a Paperwhite with a color E Ink screen. Book covers, comics, children's books and highlights show up in color, and it has a warm light, IPX8 waterproofing and a dark mode. Amazon says the 2026 version has a redesigned display with edge-to-edge color and better clarity.

Keep your expectations realistic, though. Color pages show at 150 ppi, half the sharpness of black-and-white text, and the colors are muted, closer to a printed newspaper than a phone screen. Battery life is also shorter, rated at up to 8 weeks. The Signature Edition (about $320) adds 32GB of storage, an aluminum body, an auto-adjusting light, dock charging and double-tap page turns.

**Who it's for:** comic and manga readers, people who read cookbooks or picture books, and readers who use a lot of colored highlights.

## What about the Kindle Scribe and Kids models?

If you want to write notes on the screen with a pen, look at the 11-inch **Kindle Scribe** or **Kindle Scribe Colorsoft** instead. They're much bigger and more expensive, with list prices of roughly $500 to $680, and neither is waterproof. For pure reading, the Paperwhite is the better buy.

The new **Kids** versions start at around $180 and come with a kid-friendly cover and a 12-month Amazon Kids+ subscription.

## Our take

**Kindle or phone for reading?**

I like to read books on a Kindle or e-reader because it's more comfortable for long reading sessions. It's lightweight and easy to carry, it has long battery life, and I can store thousands of books on one device. Its screen is also designed to be easier on the eyes than a regular phone screen. For occasional reading, though, a phone is more convenient.

**Is the Colorsoft worth $90 more than the Paperwhite?**

If you mostly read novels and regular books, the Kindle Paperwhite is enough. Its text is sharp, and its battery lasts longer. The extra $90 for the Colorsoft is only worth it if you read a lot of comics, magazines or illustrated books.

**Is it worth paying $20 to remove ads?**

For me, paying $20 extra to remove ads isn't worth it, because the ads don't disturb the reading experience much. I'd rather save that $20.

> **Tip:** Don't pay full price if you can wait a few weeks. Kindles are usually discounted around Black Friday and Cyber Monday, so check the price history before you buy and make sure a "deal" is on the 2026 model, not last year's.

## Should you buy a 2024 Paperwhite instead?

Probably not. TechRadar points out that the 2024 Paperwhite now sells at the same list price as the 2026 model, which is thinner and lighter. A 2024 model only makes sense if you find it at a real discount or prefer one of its colors.

## The bottom line

For most readers, the **Kindle Paperwhite** is the one to buy. It's about $50 more than the basic Kindle but adds a bigger screen, a warm light, waterproofing and twice the battery life. Pick the **basic Kindle** if you're on a tight budget and read mostly indoors, and the **Colorsoft** only if color really matters to you, such as for comics or picture books. The Signature Editions are nice upgrades but not must-haves.
